ED side-room prioritisation

All UHB emergency departments · Who gets the side room first when rooms run out · Based on NIPCM (England) and Appendix 11 v1.1 (Mar 2026)

No negative-pressure rooms in our EDs

Airborne: single room, door shut, patient masked, staff FFP3.

Care before rooms

Never delay resuscitation or hide a deteriorating patient.

Isolate on suspicion

Don't wait for results. Mask anyone with respiratory symptoms.

1 Suspected HCIDSTOP. Not covered by this sheet.
  • VHF, MERS, avian flu, novel pathogen + travel or exposure
  • Nearest single room, door shut, minimal staff
  • Trust HCID pathway: call on-call infection consultant and UKHSA HPT now

Who

VHF, MERS, avian influenza, novel pathogen: compatible illness plus travel or exposure.

Room, PPE and notes

Nearest single room, door shut, fewest staff. Do not use this sheet or the NHS tool. Trust HCID pathway (Identify · Isolate · Inform): on-call infection consultant and UKHSA HPT. Clade I mpox is no longer an HCID (ACDP, Mar 2025).

2 AirborneSingle room, door shut, patient masked, staff FFP3. Call IPC.
  • Measles: fever + rash; exposure, travel or non-immune
  • Chickenpox, disseminated or immunocompromised zoster, unexplained vesicular rash
  • Probable pulmonary TB: cough >2–3 wk, weight loss, night sweats, abnormal CXR, risk factors
  • Possible MDR-TB: escalate to micro/ID for transfer (needs negative pressure)
  • Mpox with respiratory symptoms or severe disease

Who

  • Measles: fever + rash, coryza or conjunctivitis; exposure, travel or non-immune
  • Chickenpox · disseminated or immunocompromised zoster · any unexplained vesicular rash until excluded
  • Probable pulmonary or laryngeal TB: cough >2–3 wk, haemoptysis, night sweats, weight loss, abnormal CXR, migrant/homeless/HIV
  • Possible MDR-TB: previous treatment, contact, high-burden country. Highest consequence.
  • Mpox with respiratory symptoms, severe disease or extensive lesions

Room, PPE and notes

  • No negative-pressure rooms in UHB EDs: single room, door shut, patient masked (FRSM); call IPC.
  • MDR/XDR-TB needs a negative-pressure room: escalate at once to micro/ID about transfer.
  • Staff: fit-tested FFP3 or hood for measles, chickenpox, TB, respiratory mpox. Disseminated zoster: Appendix 11 lists FRSM, FFP3 for AGPs.
  • Isolate at a low level of suspicion: one missed case exposes the waiting room.
  • Already in a shared area: tell IPC and the HPT (contact tracing, PEP).
3 EntericSingle room with en-suite or own commode. Soap and water, not gel.
  • Active vomiting ± diarrhoea (norovirus-like): top of this priority
  • C. difficile suspected or confirmed
  • Other infective gastroenteritis

Uncontained enteric shedding

Who

  • Active vomiting ± diarrhoea (norovirus-like): top of this priority. One episode in a bay can close the bay or department.
  • C. difficile suspected or confirmed: watery stool, Bristol 6–7, ≥3 in 24 h, recent antibiotics or hospital stay
  • Other infective gastroenteritis: Salmonella, STEC/O157, rotavirus, hepatitis A (single en-suite in App 11)

Room, PPE and notes

  • Single room with en-suite or dedicated commode.
  • Isolate at symptom onset, not when the result returns.
  • Contact precautions. Soap and water for hands, not gel.
  • FRSM if vomiting; no other RPE.
  • Where possible keep en-suite rooms for enteric cases.
4 High-consequence droplet / contactSingle room strongly preferred.
  • Meningococcal disease, invasive group A strep: until established on antibiotics
  • Pertussis, diphtheria
  • CPE known or high risk (higher if incontinent or wounds) · Candida auris* · crusted scabies
  • Mpox without respiratory symptoms · PVL S. aureus

Who

  • Meningococcal disease (or meningitis of unknown cause / septicaemia) and invasive group A strep
  • Pertussis · diphtheria (risk to infants and unvaccinated contacts)
  • CPE known or high risk. Top of this priority if incontinent, with wounds or uncontrolled secretions.
  • Candida auris‡ · crusted scabies†
  • Mpox without respiratory symptoms, severe disease or extensive lesions
  • PVL S. aureus (single en-suite)

Room, PPE and notes

  • Single room strongly preferred; en-suite for contact organisms.
  • Bacterial infections: until established on appropriate antimicrobials. Reviews use ~24 h; App 11 gives no hour count, so follow local policy.
  • CPE: single en-suite in App 11.
  • C. auris ranks alongside CPE in the reviews, but App 11 asks for a room only in high-risk settings (ICU, oncology/haematology).
  • Droplet: FRSM within 1 m; FFP3/hood for AGPs. Contact (CPE, C. auris): gloves/apron per risk assessment, no RPE.
5 Respiratory virusesRoom if free; otherwise cohort same confirmed virus, ≥1 m apart.
  • Influenza, COVID-19, RSV: rapid test early, then cohort
  • Move up if AGP (NIV, high-flow), uncontrolled cough or can't mask
  • Other droplet infections (mumps, rubella, adenovirus, Mycoplasma etc.)

Common respiratory viruses etc.

Who

  • Influenza, COVID-19, RSV
  • Other droplet infections‡ (single en-suite in App 11): mumps, rubella, adenovirus, parainfluenza, Mycoplasma, EV-D68, pneumococcal/Hib pneumonia (until on antimicrobials), parvovirus B19 (only before the rash)

Room, PPE and notes

  • Single room if free; otherwise cohort by confirmed pathogen: ≥1 m apart, door or bay closed, FRSM on patients.
  • Rapid PCR/POCT early. Untested: a spare room while waiting, then move on the result.
  • Move up to a single room if AGP (NIV, high-flow O₂), uncontrolled cough, or cannot mask or comply (delirium, dementia, young child).
  • Never cohort unconfirmed or different infections, immunosuppressed patients, or patients unlikely to comply. Mixed cohorts only under a Board-assured preparedness plan.
6 LowestBay acceptable once rooms run out. Discuss with IPC.
  • MRSA, ESBL, VRE carriage: continent, no wounds, no shedding
  • Localised covered shingles · extrapulmonary TB (pulmonary excluded)
  • Not infectious: known IBD flare, laxatives, psoriasis; chronic dry cough, no TB risk, normal CXR

Lowest (bay acceptable)

Who

  • MRSA, ESBL, VRE carriage‡: continent, no wound, diarrhoea or respiratory shedding. (One review would add continent CPE carriers; App 11 says single en-suite: follow local IPC.)
  • Localised, covered shingles (immunocompetent)
  • Extrapulmonary TB with pulmonary disease excluded and no draining lesion
  • Pseudomonas, Acinetobacter, Serratia, Stenotrophomonas, Pneumocystis, B. cereus: room only in high-risk settings (ICU, oncology/haematology) per App 11

Room, PPE and notes

  • Bay with standard ± contact precautions once rooms are exhausted.
  • This is rationing, not a claim that no room is needed. Discuss with IPC.
  • Not a reason for a room: diarrhoea or rash with a known non-infectious cause (IBD flare, laxatives, IBS, psoriasis); chronic dry cough with no TB risk features and a normal CXR (mask, main ED).
P ProtectiveParallel to the ladder, not last.
  • Neutropenia (<0.5 × 10⁹/L), transplant, severe immunosuppression
  • Agree with senior and haemato-oncology. Never next to infectious patients.

Protective (parallel)

Who

Neutropenia (<0.5 × 10⁹/L), post-transplant, severe immunosuppression.

Room, PPE and notes

Single room, door shut, apart from infectious patients. The room protects them from the department, so do not rank them last by default. Agree with the senior and haemato-oncology. Never bay with infectious patients or cohort with a different infection.

* Appendix 11 asks for a room for C. auris only in high-risk settings; see the full detail under priority 4.
† reviews only, not in App 11 · ‡ placed by analogy (App 11 gives room type, no rank)

No side room available? Bridge the gap

  • Mask the patient (FRSM) if tolerated. Not always appropriate on oxygen, if distressed, or in some paediatric and mental health settings.
  • Out of the waiting room: ≥1 m from others, best-ventilated, most remote space. Assess infectious patients first.
  • Airborne suspect: masked, separate, well-ventilated space; tell IPC now.
  • Vomiting or diarrhoea: own cubicle and commode, contact precautions; expect to close the area for cleaning afterwards.
  • Dedicated equipment, hand hygiene, door closed wherever there is one.
  • Cohort only ≥2 patients with the same confirmed infection, unless the Board-assured winter plan says otherwise.

A room is not free until it has been cleaned.

Triage screen

Fever + rash · vomiting/diarrhoea · cough + TB risk · travel · previous MDRO/CPE · hospital stay abroad. Mask anyone with respiratory symptoms. Isolate on suspicion, not on the result.

Two patients both need the last side room

Apply in order. Both patients keep the precautions their infection needs.

  1. Clinical safety first. Resuscitation and visibility come before isolation. Isolated patients get less contact and more supportive-care failures.
  2. Match the room to the need. Airborne suspects need a closed door away from the waiting area. Enteric cases gain most from an en-suite. Two rooms, one en-suite: the en-suite goes to the enteric case.
  3. Higher priority is allocated the room. 2 before 3, 3 before 4, and so on. Priority 1 (HCID) follows its own pathway.
  4. Same priority: likelihood × consequence. Likely MDR-TB before probable drug-sensitive TB. Vomiting or incontinent norovirus before continent C. difficile.
  5. Who cannot be contained? Uncontrolled cough, incontinence, draining wound, AGP, cannot mask, cannot comply.
  6. Who can be cohorted? Allocate the room to the patient whose infection cannot be cohorted (unconfirmed, no matching bay). PCR-confirmed flu can go to the flu bay.
  7. Who leaves the room soonest? If one patient's rapid test is due within the hour, keep them masked and apart until the result.
  8. Plan for the patient remaining in the main department. Oncology, transplant, pregnant, neonatal or unvaccinated children nearby lower the threshold to isolate. Plan their space, precautions and neighbours.
  9. Free a room. Review current occupants: de-isolate anyone cleared, resolved, non-infectious or Priority 6; move patients waiting for beds to wards.
  10. Document and escalate. Record the decision and review date in the notes and handover. Escalate to the site manager and IPC/microbiology. Incident report if a Priority 2 patient cannot be isolated.

How suspicious is enough?

Pre-test probability thresholds.

C. difficile20–30%
CPEKnown positive: always. Suspected: 5–10%

  • Basis: two reviews that broadly agree. Expert opinion, not validated. Adjust for how many rooms are free; agree local changes with IPC.
  • Expect over-isolation for TB. In one ED study only 25% of patients suspected of pulmonary TB had it, and a third of true cases were missed. About 3 in 4 isolated "TB suspects" being negative is the price of catching the rest.
  • Use a structured TB assessment (risk factors, CXR, rapid NAAT/GeneXpert) so patients leave the room quickly when the answer is no.
  • Not adopted: one review isolates only above 70% (10–70% wait in a cubicle for testing). This would leave suspected airborne infection in an open cubicle, and Appendix 11 specifies placement for suspected cases.

Vital numbers and notification

  • 1 m minimum separation of infectious patients awaiting assessment or cohorted, door closed (NIPCM ch.2).
  • FFP3 (or hood) must be fit-tested and fit-checked each time; never worn by the patient. Staff without a fit test: hood.
  • AGPs on a patient with suspected or confirmed droplet/airborne infection: FFP3 or hood, and a room if possible.
  • Notify on suspicion: measles, TB, meningococcal, mumps, rubella, pertussis, diphtheria, mpox, hepatitis A, invasive GAS and others marked "Yes" in App 11. Phone the HPT within 24 h if urgent; written notification within 3 days. Don't wait for the lab.
  • Exposure in a shared area: tell IPC and HPT.

NHS England Isolation Prioritisation Tool (v1.0, Oct 2024)

  • Scores risk = probability × impact for one pathogen in one patient population. It does not rank pathogens and does not decide.
  • "Isolation recommended? Yes/No" is a manual entry, then free text for reasoning, review date and de-isolation date.
  • Meant for a multidisciplinary team (IPC, medical, nursing, operations, facilities). Never for a suspected HCID.
  • Use it when two patients at the same priority both need a room, when the organism isn't on the priorities list, or to document a decision.

Probability (stop at the first "No")

  1. Population at risk of further exposure?
  2. Susceptible?
  3. Highly transmissible, environmentally persistent, widely spread or antimicrobial-resistant?
  4. Transmission likely?

Probability: not yet answered

Impact (up to 4 questions)

  • Severe disease after exposure?
  • Significant number of the wider population susceptible or at risk?
  • Negative effect on current service and future capacity?
  • Effective treatment and controls in place or possible?

This sheet does not say how the four impact answers combine into a level, so choose the impact level the team agrees.

Status of this guidance

NIPCM and Appendix 11 give the room type for each infection but no national pathogen-by-pathogen priority order. The NHS England Isolation Prioritisation Tool ranks by risk, not by pathogen. The priorities here are a synthesis of three evidence reviews: consensus, not mandated. Trust IPC policy overrides this sheet; ask IPC or on-call microbiology if unsure.

Quality of the evidence

  • Strong: airborne organisms spread across shared airspace; masking, separating and rapid testing help.
  • Weak: that single rooms themselves reduce transmission (low or very low certainty for every organism; no randomised trials; mostly bundled outbreak measures). Isolation has real harms: anxiety, depression, less clinician contact, possibly more adverse events.
  • Expert opinion only: every numeric threshold and the order within priorities.
  • The reviews' epidemiology figures disagree in places (e.g. TB notifications) and two cite non-authoritative web sources (e.g. a vendor blog, Substack), so no incidence figures are used on this sheet.
